*Welcome to Beyond Breaking In*

There's a question that follows me everywhere: conferences, mentoring sessions, tech panels. It always finds me:

🎙️ *"What would you tell someone trying to break into tech?"*

I used to have a ready answer. Study this. Contribute to OSS.  Learn that. Network here. Know this programming language. But lately, I've been sitting with the question differently. Not because I don't care about it anymore, but because the question itself feels like it belongs to a different time.

For a long time, tech was a destination. We were told this was the PLACE. A walled garden with its own gatekeepers and a very specific idea of who belonged. As a CS major graduate, where and how I got my degree always comes up over 10 years later. 

If you hadn’t coded since you were a kid, you were out. If you couldn't, you were on the outside looking in. But still being told you should BREAK IN. And I witnessed so many spend their energy trying to convince people to let them in.

But something has shifted. What we need to know has changed. How we build has changed. It is about being able to express yourself in the form that works best for you. It's about taste, intent, and understanding who you want to build for. With simplification and code access, we have seen a new wave of creators emerge.

I've always believed that technology should be accessible to everyone. I entered the industry to do just that. And I DID IT! From online dev experiences to the simplification of entire frameworks and languages. But now we're finally starting to live in that reality.  I am not saying that my traditional CS experience was unworthy; it just changed. Tools of creation, code, have become more accessible. Now, I am not sure everyone can build a 1B business of 1; they may be able to build a sustainable business of a few. The tools are in everyone's hands. Code is a tool for all of us. 

So my advice has changed. I've stopped telling people how to *join* the industry and started telling them to *redefine* it.

Think about it: if the tools are available to everyone, why are we still asking for permission to enter someone else's space? Why not build our own? Instead of looking for a seat at an existing table, use your unique perspective, your background, your community, and your lived experience to build a better one. 

*Where I take a beat: Do I worry about who is in charge and if they will create an equal opportunity ABSOLUTELY. The unsettled feeling you feel isn't only about the tech emerging, it's about the people in charge. Hope feels lost. I would feel a little more hope if I had more leaders in AI who spoke with care, or politicians who gave us hope; it would feel a little better. But in times when you feel like you're being led into the future by greed, it hits harder. It makes you feel lost. It reminds me of when I went to university at the start of the recession. You felt lost and may feel the same now.*

I think we are in the time of builders. Not just people who write code, but people who see problems in their world and now have the means to solve them. A teacher who builds a tool for their classroom. A small business owner who automates a workflow. A student in my home city of Kampala who ships an app that solves a problem no one in Silicon Valley ever thought about, because they don't think about us. Can we build our ecosystem with taste makers and experts who understand what needs to be done VS what serves the bottom line?

But! And this matters; we can't rush forward without looking back. I understand speed to market, and it will happen with AI. But that's not what I am talking about.  The first waves of tech didn't get it right. It moved fast, broke things, and often left people behind. If we're entering a new era where everyone can build, we also have a responsibility to ask harder questions. Who benefits? Who's left out? What are we optimizing for, and at what cost?

The question isn't just *how do I break into tech?* anymore. It's bigger than that.

*How do we build ethically, thoughtfully,  and with taste for everyone, without repeating the mistakes of the past?* 

That's the question I'm carrying with me now. And I think it's the one worth spending time on. 

*Welcome to Beyond Breaking In. Notes to past me to future you*
